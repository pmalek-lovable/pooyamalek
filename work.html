Decisions, not just screens.

Two problems where the interesting part was the thinking. Confidential product detail is left out on purpose. What matters is how the call got made, which is the same thing I teach.

Eurowings Digital · Product Design Lead Systems

Making the post-booking partner experience scale.

The problem

Every new partner integration in the post-booking flow was designed from scratch. Six-plus products, inconsistent patterns, and a team solving the same problem slightly differently each time.

The decision

Stop designing integrations one by one. Build a shared model for how partner and ancillary experiences behave, so the next integration starts from a system instead of a blank canvas.

Why it mattered

The interesting part wasn't the UI. It was convincing the team that consistency was a design decision worth defending, and turning that belief into something reusable that outlived any single project.

B2C e-commerce · Senior Product Designer Conversion

Reducing friction where the stakes felt high.

The problem

Users hesitated at a high-commitment step. The instinct on the team was to redesign the screen. The data suggested the screen wasn't the issue, the sequence and the framing were.

The decision

Change what the user is asked to decide, and when, instead of restyling the page. Make the commitment feel proportional to where they were in the journey.

Why it mattered

It's the difference between treating a symptom and finding the cause. The redesign everyone wanted would have looked better and changed nothing. Naming the real problem is the senior move.
