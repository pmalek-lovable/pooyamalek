Hi, I'm Pooya. For thirteen years I've designed the products people use to book flights, find homes, and buy things online. Somewhere along the way I realized most good designers aren't stuck on skill. They're stuck on clarity. So now I spend part of my time helping them find it. Glad you're here.

The questions I get asked most

Straight answers, the way I'd give them over coffee.

You're ready when you can explain the decision, not just show the screen. If you can say why the call was right, what you gave up, and what happened because of it, you're already working at senior. Most people wait to be told. You don't have to.

Because good feedback measures your work, and promotion measures how visible your thinking is. Those are different things. You're probably handing over outcomes when the people deciding need to see the judgment behind them. That gap is fixable, and usually faster than you'd expect.

It probably has ten screens and no decisions. Nobody hiring you is buying your pixels, they're buying how you think. So cut the tour. Show one hard call, why you made it, and what changed because of it. One honest decision beats ten polished mockups.

Usually you're trying to remember everything instead of telling one thing well. Pick a single project and practice telling it until the decision, not the design, is the point. Confidence comes from knowing your own story, not from having more slides.
